# The Stolen Minute — original Arabic sample

Mira wants to enter a robot competition, but the shortcut steals the memory that
makes her friendship recognizable. She chooses to return the borrowed minute and
loses the competition, earning back the three-tap greeting introduced by the film.
This is original fiction for ages 13–17, not a user's supplied or historical story.

The same Arabic narration is directed in three ways:

- Collage Memory: large black-and-white halftone photographic cutouts, crisp cream
  edges, bold teal/mustard/coral paper fields, tactile assembly and a red ticket
  carrying scene changes. Keep 3–6 meaningful groups per shot.
- Clawd Memory: original brush-drawn characters with acted faces, anticipation,
  contact, asymmetrical poses, distinct views and restrained 12Hz line boil.
  Use the downloaded Clawd guide's animation principles; these are our characters.
- Paper Theatre: a lit three-dimensional pop-up book, flat paper actors and sets,
  real shadows, hinge reveals, macro camera coverage and page turns. Lemo's local
  paper-popup guide supplies the medium; no demo plot, assets or music are copied.

Narration is generated first and sets timing. Action and reaction get separate
reads; no paragraph title cards or generic idle drift. Captions are overlays.
Each draft is at least one minute, 1440×2560 at 60fps. Character IDs and world rules
are locked in series/mira-minute/bible.json, with episode outcomes kept separately.
The user's authorization and free/code workflow override upstream paid video models,
automatic setup and intermediate approval pauses.
